# Research

Research completed 2026-08-30. T1 denotes original scholarship or an official
technical source; T2 denotes an authoritative synthesis. No general-web lead is
used as evidence. The threshold is treated as an object of inquiry, not a result.

## Findings

- [T2] IPCC AR6 WGII assesses compound and cascading risks with high confidence
  and describes transmission through trade, finance, food, and ecosystems. It
  also assesses increasing risk of simultaneous crop losses with warming. WGII
  Summary for Policymakers B.5, Technical Summary TS.C.11.6, and chapters 5, 7,
  and 16. Supports the model class, not the billion-death threshold.
- [T1] Kemp and coauthors argue that catastrophic climate scenarios are
  comparatively underexplored and propose an agenda spanning mass mortality,
  systemic vulnerability, and societal fragility. *PNAS* 119(34), e2108146119,
  DOI 10.1073/pnas.2108146119. It is a perspective, not a probability estimate.
- [T1] Arnscheidt and coauthors distinguish hazard, vulnerability, systemic
  amplification, and latent risk. Their mortality anchor for global catastrophe
  is at least 10% of the global population. *Global Sustainability* 8, e19, DOI
  10.1017/sus.2025.20. Supports causal decomposition and threshold context.
- [T1] Linkov and coauthors model trade and sociopolitical networks in the Late
  Bronze Age. Single-node disruptions do not reproduce collapse, while some
  paired disruptions generate cascades. *Global Environmental Change* 84,
  102792, DOI 10.1016/j.gloenvcha.2023.102792. Supports interaction effects and
  warns against using 1177 BCE as a direct quantitative template for the modern
  world.
- [T1] Kornhuber and coauthors show that climate and crop models can
  underestimate synchronized low-yield risk linked to atmospheric circulation.
  *Nature Communications* 14, 3528, DOI 10.1038/s41467-023-38906-7. Supports a
  model-discrepancy layer.
- [T1] Gaupp and coauthors estimate changing probabilities of simultaneous
  global breadbasket failure under climate change. *Nature Climate Change* 10,
  54-57, DOI 10.1038/s41558-019-0600-z. Supports joint-yield dependence rather
  than independent national shocks.
- [T1] Hultgren and coauthors estimate climate impacts across 12,658 regions and
  six staple crops while accounting for observed adaptation. Their central
  aggregate conversion is an annual loss of 5.5e14 kilocalories per degree of
  warming, about 120 kilocalories per person per day or 4.4% of recommended
  consumption. *Nature* 642, 644-652, DOI 10.1038/s41586-025-09085-w. Supports
  production forcing but cannot be read directly as mortality.
- [T1] Bevacqua and coauthors show that some model outcomes at 2 C exceed
  multimodel-average impacts at 4 C for selected extremes, including a
  breadbasket drought metric. *Nature* 651, 946-953, DOI
  10.1038/s41586-026-10237-9. Supports explicit structural and model uncertainty.
- [T1] Kuhla, Puma, and Otto combine a wheat trade network with a market model.
  Their counterfactuals show that production losses and escalating export
  restrictions can compound; cooperation and favorable harvests reduced the
  modeled 2022 price shock. *Communications Earth & Environment* 5, 481, DOI
  10.1038/s43247-024-01638-7. Supports endogenous policy response and model
  validation against a recent stress event.
- [T1] Puma and coauthors measure increasing fragility in the international food
  trade network and identify vulnerabilities linked to concentration and
  dependency. *Environmental Research Letters* 10, 024007, DOI
  10.1088/1748-9326/10/2/024007. Supports temporal network construction.
- [T1] Xia and coauthors couple climate, crop, fishery, and food-allocation
  models under nuclear-war soot scenarios. They estimate mortality above 2
  billion and 5 billion in specified scenarios. *Nature Food* 3, 586-596, DOI
  10.1038/s43016-022-00573-0. This is a structural analogue for a severe global
  food shock, not an estimate transferable to greenhouse warming.
- [T1] Buldyrev and coauthors show analytically that dependencies across networks
  can create recursive and abrupt failures that single-network analysis misses.
  *Nature* 464, 1025-1028, DOI 10.1038/nature08932. Supports the multilayer
  architecture; its percolation thresholds cannot be imported as empirical
  thresholds for states or food systems.
- [T2] Boccaletti and coauthors review multilayer network theory and distinguish
  multiplex, interconnected, and interdependent structures. *Physics Reports*
  544(1), 1-122, DOI 10.1016/j.physrep.2014.07.001. Supports notation and layer
  choices.
- [T1] Au and Beck develop subset simulation, replacing direct estimation of a
  tiny failure probability with a product of more estimable conditional
  probabilities. *Probabilistic Engineering Mechanics* 16(4), 263-277, DOI
  10.1016/S0266-8920(01)00019-4. Supports the proposed tail-search method.
- [T2] The National Academies define factual and counterfactual probabilities
  and risk ratios for event attribution. *Attribution of Extreme Weather Events
  in the Context of Climate Change* (2016), DOI 10.17226/21852. Supports paired
  ensembles while underscoring that attribution confidence varies by event.
- [T1] The UN's *World Population Prospects 2024* reports 8.2 billion people in
  2024. A billion deaths therefore correspond to roughly 12.2% of that reference
  population. This arithmetic is a scale check, not a projection.
- [T1] IPC Technical Manual version 3.1 requires convergent evidence for an area
  famine classification, including at least 20% of households in catastrophic
  food conditions, at least 30% acute malnutrition among children under five by
  the specified measure, and a crude non-trauma death rate at or above 2 per
  10,000 per day. The death rate is a calibration yardstick, not a global
  mortality equation.
- [T2] WHO states that undernutrition increases vulnerability to disease and
  death, especially among children. Supports a coupled nutrition-infection
  hazard rather than counting starvation and infection independently.

## Corrections to the origin conversation

- There is no defensible published probability of a climate-caused
  billion-death year. The paper does not invent one.
- The principal scientifically constructible pathway is multi-system
  amplification. This is not a claim that direct heat, fire, flood, or storm
  mortality is negligible.
- A historical collapse analogy contributes topology and sequencing. It does
  not provide modern parameter values.
- Nuclear-winter results show what integrated food-shock modeling can produce
  under a radically different forcing. They cannot be scaled to greenhouse
  warming.
- Sectoral death estimates overlap. The proposed model assigns death through
  competing hazards with individual or cohort state, preventing simple sums.
- The maximum rolling-year outcome may occur years after the initiating climate
  shocks. The simulation horizon must therefore be longer than 365 days.
- Calibration must include systems that absorbed large shocks without global
  collapse. Tuning only to failures would make catastrophe inevitable by design.
